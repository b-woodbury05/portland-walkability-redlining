# Redlining and Walkability in Portland, Oregon

Do neighborhoods that were redlined in the 1930s have lower
walkability today? This project tests that question across 68 Portland
neighborhoods using regression analysis in R.

[Read the full report (PDF)](report/Legacy%20in%20the%20Lines.pdf)

## Key findings
- Contrary to my hypothesis, formerly redlined neighborhoods (Grades
  B–D) scored roughly 20–46 points higher on walkability than
  Grade A neighborhoods, across all models.
- Formerly redlined neighborhoods being more walkable does not mean that communities originally harmed by redlining are the ones receiving the benefits of walkability. Direct city planning and indirect gentrification has displaced many people of color from their historical neighborhoods, so that they cannot benefit from the positives of modern urban density. 
- This study found evidence that within similarly graded neighborhoods, people of
color are associated with lower walkability scores which means that walkability is not a neutral metric and people of color do not have equal access to it.
